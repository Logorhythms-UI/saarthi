## The problem

Dementia reduces memory, thinking, and the ability to do daily tasks. The World Health Organization (WHO) gives these numbers:

- About 57 million people lived with dementia in 2021.
- About 10 million new cases occur each year.
- The global cost of dementia was about US$1.3 trillion in 2019. About half of this cost comes from informal care.

A common early sign is that the person puts everyday objects in the wrong place. Keys, glasses, wallets, and medicine are typical examples. The person then asks a caregiver for help. This loss of independence causes stress.

## Why current tools do not work

| Limit | Effect |
|-------|--------|
| Trackers and alarms react to events. | They give no help to find an object. |
| Smartphone apps need a screen. | Patients find complex screens hard to use. |
| Many systems need the cloud. | They fail when the network is weak, and they risk the privacy of the user. |
| Lifelogging cameras give a timeline. | The user must browse the timeline by hand. |
| Heavy models run on the wearable. | The wearable is large and the battery is short. |

Related systems include SenseCam, GO-Finder, Anotoki Camera, CamFi, MemPal, and VizWiz. Each one has at least one of these limits.

## The gap that Saarthi fills

No known system combines all of these features:

1. Continuous, local visual memory.
2. Understanding of both images and language.
3. A light wearable with a stronger home hub.
4. Offline operation.

## Target users

The primary users are people with mild to moderate dementia. These people keep many abilities. They want to live without constant help. A tool that works as an external memory can support them.

The secondary users are caregivers and family members.

## What clinicians said

The team talked with medical experts. The clinicians gave these answers:

- Short-term memory loss, getting lost, and word-finding problems are the most frustrating difficulties.
- Misplaced objects cause agitation in most patients. They cause more than mild frustration.
- All clinicians said that patients find standard screen interfaces hard to use.
- Five of six clinicians said that mild and moderate patients speak in a fragmented way. The language processing must accept broken sentences.
- Most clinicians rated an always-available object finder as highly or extremely effective.
- Clinicians disagreed about spoken descriptions of the surroundings. Some said they help. Others said they can startle the patient.

<Note title="Design effect">
These answers led to a voice-first design with low mental load. They also led to a query step that accepts broken sentences.
</Note>
